Demand Step A: Define a collective heating demand cluster
=========================================================

The total heating demand for a large number of nearby consumers can be aggregated and
represented by a collective heating demand cluster.

This can be a useful method for the draft design of an integral heating system (i.e.
from source up to consumers), as it avoids drawing every pipe line connecting to each
individual consumer and also speeds up calculations.

In this Step by step, we explain how to apply this method for 4th generation heating
networks, where the primary and secondary grids (the distribution grid) are separate
hydraulical systems that connect at the so-called heating transfer substations

Nonetheless, the designer can also define collective heating demand clusters that
aggregate demand at another spatial level, for instance for a street, a municipality or
a region, depending on the system scale and design exercise at hand.

1. Draw the polygon for the demand area for which the heat demand is aggregated


2. Place the heating transfer substation


3. Specify the maximum capacity for the heating transfer substation


4. Specify the CAPEX costs of the secondary system between the heating transfer
   substation and building connections


5. Specify the dynamic heating demand:


   * Option 1:


      * Use one of the standard profiles available


      * Scale this profile with the maximum capacity


   * Option 2:


      * Use a user-specified profile of the heating demand over the year


      * The user can also make use of the heat profile generator to create profiles
